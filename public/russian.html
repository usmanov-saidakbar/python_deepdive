Russian: B2 speaking in 10 months

A 10-month plan built on free resources only: 5 stages in order, from A1 to B2, with a speaking track, an input track and recorded speaking projects running all year. Every topic has a target depth; tick the steps it needs (Learn, Drill, Speak, Automatic) as you go. Your notes are saved with your checklist.

Overall progress
How to use this roadmap

Goal: speak Russian at B2 in 10 months. In CEFR terms, B2 speaking means you can talk with native speakers fluently and spontaneously enough that it's no strain for either side, take an active part in discussions, and explain your view on a current issue with the advantages and disadvantages of each option.

The honest numbers: from zero, B2 in Russian takes roughly 700 hours or more. The US Foreign Service Institute puts Russian in Category III: about 1,100 class hours to professional working proficiency, a level above B2. This plan is about 2 focused hours plus 30–60 minutes of passive listening a day, 6 days a week, which comes to roughly 650–750 hours in 10 months. If you already understand some Russian, you need less; at under 12 hours a week, plan for 13–15 months instead.

Words you need (the official ТРКИ lexical minimums): A1 780 · A2 about 1,300 · B1 2,300 · B2 about 5,100. You need to understand most of them and to use about half.

Depth levels (the badge next to each topic decides which steps it needs):

LevelMeaningDone when
RecognizeYou understand it when you hear or read itLearn and Spot: you found and understood 10 real examples
UseYou can say it correctly when you take a second to thinkLearn, Drill, Speak: 10+ sentences of your own, and you used it in a conversation
AutomaticIt comes out right in unplanned speechAll four steps: a whole conversation went by without a correction on it

A 2-hour day (Paul Nation's four strands: input, output, language study and fluency, each about a quarter of the time):

BlockTimeWhat you do
1. Anki15 minReviews, then new cards: phrases and sentences with audio and stress
2. Grammar20 minThe week's topic: Learn, then Drill out loud
3. Focused input25 minListening or reading at your level, with a transcript (see the Input track)
4. Shadowing10 minSpeak along with 1–2 minutes of that audio
5. Speaking40 minA conversation, or a 4/3/2 talk with a recording (weekly rhythm below)
6. Error log10 minWrite down today's corrections and turn them into Anki cards

Plus 30–60 minutes of passive listening: commute, gym, chores. Short day? Do blocks 1, 4 and 5 (65 minutes).

When to move on: a Use topic once you've said it in 10 sentences of your own and in one conversation; an Automatic topic once a whole conversation goes by without a correction on it. If a topic takes twice the planned time, put it in your error log and move on: every later stage recycles cases, aspect and motion verbs.

Two risks: collecting instead of speaking (apps, grammar videos and Anki feel productive but don't build speaking: if the weekly check shows under 3 hours of speaking, cut grammar time, not speaking), and fluent but wrong (speaking a lot with the same mistakes fixes them in place: the error log and the Automatic step catch this).

Every 2 months, redo the Stage 0 self-assessment and record the same 2-minute answer, then adjust the plan to your real pace.

Weekly check

Sunday, 20 minutes. Answer five questions for this week:

  • How many minutes did I speak Russian? (Target: 210, about 30 a day. Under 180? Next week, cut grammar time, not speaking.)
  • How many hours did I listen? (Target: 7.)
  • Which topic reached Use or Automatic?
  • Which 3 mistakes came up most in my error log?
  • What can I say now that I couldn't say last week?
